# Bridge: Insect Agent Atlas → Lineage Recursive Improvement

The Insect Agent Atlas was built to expand agent-architecture search space using mechanisms abstracted from insect ecology. One recurring implication is that useful intelligence need not be organized as a single durable individual. Colonies, castes, lifecycle transitions, stigmergy, distributed roles, reproduction, and environmental modification suggest alternative units of computation and adaptation.

On 2026-09-11, Lu Cheng articulated a narrower research question from this direction:

> If recursive self-improvement is usually imagined as one agent repeatedly improving itself, what changes when the recursive unit is instead a lineage or population that can generate, select, and differentiate descendants?

This question is now tracked as **Lineage Recursive Improvement (LRI)** in:

`../lineage-recursive-improvement/`

## Important boundary

The Atlas is not evidence that biological evolution maps directly to AI engineering. LRI is not claimed as a proven consequence of insect systems. The biological material is a source of architectural priors; the LRI claim must stand or fall through controlled computational experiments.

Existing research already covers evolutionary archives, group-evolving agents, artificial agent ecologies, and recursive descent. LRI therefore makes no claim to have invented population-based agent evolution. Its purpose is to keep a particular research axis explicit and testable: **lineage/ecology-level recursion as an alternative to assuming that recursive improvement should converge into one persistent self.**
